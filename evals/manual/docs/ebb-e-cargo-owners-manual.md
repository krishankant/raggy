# Ebb E-Cargo owner's manual

Edition 3, for frames with serial numbers starting EC26. Brightwater Bikes, assembled at the Fenwick workshop.

Read this manual before your first ride, and keep it with the bike. Sections that matter for safety are marked "Safety".

## 1. Safety

The Ebb E-Cargo is an electrically assisted cargo bike for one rider and up to two children or cargo in the front box. The motor only assists while you pedal, and assistance stops at 25 km/h.

Always wear a helmet. Check the brakes, the tyre pressure and the box latches before every ride. Never ride with the box lid unlatched: at speed the lid can open into the front wheel.

Do not wash the bike with a pressure washer. Water forced past the seals can reach the motor connector and the battery contacts. Use a bucket, a soft brush and low water pressure.

Do not modify the motor controller or fit a different battery. Doing so voids the warranty and may make the bike illegal to ride on public roads.

Children in the box must wear helmets and be strapped in with the included five-point harnesses. Children must be at least 9 months old and able to sit upright unaided.

## 2. In the box

Your Ebb arrives fully built. Inside the shipping crate you will also find:

- the battery (500 Wh), packed separately in a protective sleeve
- the charger (4 A) with a 1.5 m mains cable
- two battery keys on a single ring, stamped with a three-digit key number
- two five-point child harnesses
- a rain cover for the box
- a 4 mm and a 5 mm hex key
- this manual and a warranty registration card

Write your key number on the warranty card. Replacement keys can only be ordered by quoting the key number.

## 3. Specifications

| Item | Value |

| Weight without battery | 35.1 kg |

| Gears | 5-speed internal hub |

The maximum total load includes the bike itself. A rider of 85 kg with the bike at 38 kg (battery fitted) can therefore carry at most 97 kg of cargo, but never more than 80 kg in the box.

## 4. Controls and display

The display sits in the middle of the handlebar. The left-hand remote has three buttons: up, down and power. Hold power for two seconds to switch the system on or off. Press up or down to change the assist level. Hold down for three seconds to switch the lights on or off.

The display shows speed, assist level, battery charge in 10% steps, and the trip distance. Press power briefly to cycle between trip distance, total distance and estimated range.

When the system detects a fault, the display shows an error code instead of the speed.

| Code | Meaning | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| E01 | Speed sensor signal lost | Check that the magnet on the rear rotor is in place and the sensor cable is plugged in |
| E02 | Motor overheated | Stop, switch off and let the motor cool for 15 minutes |
| E03 | Battery temperature out of range | Move the battery somewhere between 10 and 30 °C before using it or charging it |
| E04 | Throttle or remote fault | Unplug and reconnect the remote cable at the display |
| E05 | Torque sensor fault | Book a service; do not ride with assistance |
| E06 | Display communication error | Switch off, remove the battery for 30 seconds, then refit it |
| E07 | Charging interrupted | See section 5, "Charging in the cold" |
| E08 | Battery cell imbalance | Fully charge the battery and leave it on the charger for two more hours |
| E09 | Firmware mismatch | Update all components; see section 15 |

If an error comes back after you have followed the instructions, write down the code and the total distance and book a service.

## 5. Battery and charging

### Charging

Charge the battery on or off the bike. The charging port is on the left side of the battery, under a rubber cap. A full charge from empty takes about 5 hours; charging from 20% to 80% takes about 2 hours.

The charger's light is red while charging and green when the battery is full. You can leave the charger connected after the light turns green; it stops charging automatically.

### Charging in the cold

The battery only accepts charge between 5 °C and 40 °C. If you bring the bike in from below 5 °C, wait at least one hour before charging, so the cells can warm up. If you charge a battery that is too cold, the charger stops and the display shows E07.

### Range

Range depends on assist level, load, hills, wind and temperature. Typical range on flat roads at 20 °C with a full battery:

| Assist level | Rider only | Rider + 80 kg cargo |
|---|---|---|
| Eco | 90 km | 70 km |
| Tour | 80 km | 60 km |
| Sport | 65 km | 55 km |
| Turbo | 55 km | 50 km |

In winter, expect about 30% less range.

### Storage

For storage longer than one month, leave the battery at 50 to 70% charge, in a dry place between 10 and 20 °C, and top it up to that level every three months. Never store the battery fully empty: a cell that drops below its minimum voltage may never charge again, and that damage is not covered by the warranty.

### Battery life

The battery keeps at least 70% of its capacity for 800 full charge cycles. A full cycle is 100% of capacity charged in total, so charging from 50% to 100% twice counts as one cycle.

## 6. Your first ride

Before your first ride, adjust the saddle height so your leg is almost straight with the pedal at the bottom. The minimum-insertion line on the seat post must stay hidden inside the frame.

Start in Eco with the box empty, on a quiet street. The Ebb is longer than a normal bike, so practise turning in a wide space and remember that the front wheel sits about 1 m ahead of where a normal bike's would be.

The kickstand is a two-legged centre stand. Always use it when loading children or cargo, and put it down only with the bike on level ground.

## 7. Assist levels

There are four assist levels plus off. Eco adds 50% of your own pedalling effort, Tour 100%, Sport 180% and Turbo 300%. The higher the level, the shorter the range.

Walk assist helps you push the loaded bike, up to 6 km/h. To use it, select any assist level, then hold the up button for two seconds while walking beside the bike.

## 8. Carrying children and cargo

The box takes up to two children on the bench seat, or cargo, or one child and cargo. The bench folds up against the side of the box when you need the floor space.

Strap each child in with a five-point harness, adjusted so you can fit only two fingers between the strap and the child's collarbone. Children's feet must rest on the box floor, not on the frame.

Heavy cargo goes as low and as far back in the box as possible, close to the rider. A load in the front of the box makes the steering heavy.

The rain cover clips onto the box edges with eight press studs. Open the front window panel on warm days: the inside of a closed cover heats up quickly in sunshine.

## 9. Maintenance schedule

Follow this schedule, whichever comes first: the distance or the time.

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Every ride | Brakes, tyre pressure, box latches |
| Every 500 km or monthly | Clean and oil the chain; check spoke tension by squeezing pairs of spokes |
| Every 1,500 km or every 6 months | Workshop service: brake pads, hub gear adjustment, steering link bolts |
| Every 5,000 km or yearly | Major service: brake fluid change, hub oil change, bearings, firmware update |

The first standard service is free if you have it done within six months of purchase.

## 10. Brakes

Both brakes are hydraulic discs. The left lever works the front brake and the right lever the rear brake. With a load in the box, use both brakes together and brake earlier than on a normal bike: stopping distance with 80 kg in the box is about 40% longer.

Replace the brake pads when the friction material is thinner than 1 mm. Use only organic compound pads, which the Fenwick workshop and Saltmarsh Cycles stock for the Ebb: sintered pads overheat the 203 mm rotors on long descents with a full load.

If a lever feels spongy or pulls back to the handlebar, stop riding and book a service. Air in the hydraulic line cannot be fixed at the roadside.

## 11. Tyres and pressure

Pressure depends on the load:

| Load | Front (20 in) | Rear (26 in) |
|---|---|---|
| Rider only | 3.0 bar | 2.5 bar |
| Rider + cargo up to 40 kg | 3.5 bar | 3.0 bar |
| Rider + cargo over 40 kg | 4.0 bar | 3.5 bar |

Never exceed the maximum pressure printed on the tyre sidewall. Check the pressure at least once a week; a cargo bike tyre loses more pressure than you can feel by squeezing it.

To fix a puncture in the rear wheel, you first need to undo the hub gear cable at the clip under the chainstay. Remember to refit the cable and check that gear 3 lines up with the yellow marks on the hub before riding.

## 12. Gears

The 5-speed hub changes gear even when you are standing still, so change down before you stop at traffic lights. Ease off the pedals for a moment while changing gear under load; the motor detects the shift and pauses its assistance for a fraction of a second.

If gears slip or jump, select gear 3 and look at the yellow marks on the hub. If they don't line up, turn the barrel adjuster on the shifter until they do. If that doesn't fix it, book a service.

## 13. Lights

The front and rear lights run from the main battery. When the battery reaches 5%, motor assistance switches off but the lights keep working for at least two more hours. Hold the down button for three seconds to switch the lights on or off.

## 14. Transport and storage

To carry the Ebb on a car rack, remove the battery first and use a rack rated for at least 40 kg per bike. Most standard towbar racks are not long enough for the bike's 2.6 m length; ask us for a list of tested racks.

On trains, check the operator's rules for long bikes before you travel. The bike does not fold.

Store the bike indoors or under cover. If you store it outside for more than a day, remove the battery and take it indoors.

## 15. Firmware updates

The motor, display and battery each have firmware. Updates fix faults and sometimes change how the assistance feels. Workshops update the firmware at every major service, and you can update it at home with the Brightwater Ride app.

To update at home:

1. Charge the battery to at least 50%.
2. Switch the system on and open the Brightwater Ride app on your phone.
3. Connect to the bike in the app: the display shows a six-digit pairing code to type in.
4. Tap "Check for updates", then "Update all". Do not ride, switch off or remove the battery during the update.
5. Wait until the app shows "All components up to date" and the display restarts by itself. This takes up to 20 minutes.

If the update stops halfway, the display may show E09. Repeat the update from step 1; your settings are not lost.

## 16. Warranty registration

Register your bike within 30 days of delivery so we can contact you about safety recalls and handle warranty claims faster. Register with the card from the box or in the Brightwater Ride app, using the frame serial number stamped under the bottom bracket.

Registration doesn't change your cover. The frame has a lifetime warranty against manufacturing defects for the original owner. The battery is covered for three years or 800 full charge cycles, whichever comes first. The motor carries its maker's two-year warranty.

## 17. Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| No assistance, display on | Assist set to off, or the battery is at 5% or less | Press up to select an assist level; charge the battery |
| Display does not switch on | Battery not seated, or fully empty | Remove and refit the battery until it clicks; charge it |
| Assistance cuts in and out | Speed sensor magnet moved | Realign the magnet on the rear rotor with the sensor (gap 2 to 5 mm) |
| Clicking noise when pedalling | Pedals or crank bolts loose | Tighten the pedals with a 15 mm spanner and the crank bolts with the 5 mm hex key |
| Steering feels heavy | Load at the front of the box, or low front tyre pressure | Move the load back; check the front tyre pressure |
| Range much lower than usual | Cold weather, low tyre pressure, or an ageing battery | Check pressure; in winter, store and charge the battery indoors |

## 18. Disposal and recycling

Do not put the battery in household waste. Brightwater takes back old batteries free of charge at both workshops and refunds 30 credits towards a new battery from us. The frame, wheels and motor can go to any metal recycling point once the battery is removed.
